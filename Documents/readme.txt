
# CRISTAR — CAN-RGX 9

## Project Documentation

This repository contains the primary project-management, proposal, design, and review documentation for **CRISTAR**, the University of Toronto team participating in the **Canadian Reduced Gravity Experiment Design Challenge (CAN-RGX 9)**.

CRISTAR investigates **laser-induced crystallization under reduced- and increased-gravity conditions**. The experiment uses a compact laser-cavitation system to generate lysozyme crystals during parabolic flight aboard the National Research Council of Canada's Falcon 20 research aircraft. The resulting crystals are characterized to investigate how gravity influences crystal morphology and structure.

CAN-RGX is a Canadian student research competition conducted in collaboration with the **National Research Council of Canada (NRC)** and the **Canadian Space Agency (CSA)**. Selected teams progress through proposal development, project management, preliminary and critical design reviews, testing, flight-readiness activities, and experimental flight operations. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## Project Overview

The central scientific question investigated by CRISTAR is:

> **How does gravity influence laser-induced crystallization?**

On Earth, buoyancy-driven convection and sedimentation can influence crystal nucleation and growth. Reduced-gravity environments provide an opportunity to investigate crystallization while substantially altering these transport mechanisms.

CRISTAR uses laser-induced cavitation to initiate crystallization in a controlled sample environment. Experiments are performed under different gravitational conditions during parabolic flight, allowing the resulting crystals to be compared across gravity regimes.

Key experimental objectives include examining differences in:

- Crystal size
- Crystal morphology
- Crystal structure
- Crystal quality and purity
- Crystallization behaviour under reduced and increased gravity

The broader objective is to evaluate whether short-duration suborbital/parabolic-flight experiments can provide useful insight into processes relevant to microgravity crystal growth while offering a more accessible experimental platform than long-duration orbital missions. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## CAN-RGX 9

CAN-RGX is an annual Canadian competition in which university and college teams design, build, test, and fly scientific experiments aboard the NRC Falcon 20 research aircraft. The aircraft performs parabolic manoeuvres that produce brief periods of near-zero gravity as well as periods of increased acceleration. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

For CAN-RGX 9, CRISTAR progressed through the competition's engineering-development framework alongside three other selected teams:

- Team CRISTAR — University of Toronto
- Team CLOT-LESS — University of British Columbia
- Waterloo Space Soldering Team — University of Waterloo
- Team CanaDune — University of Waterloo :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
